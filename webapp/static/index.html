Why the shapes differ

FCFS and EDF fill ports in the order EVs arrive or depart, so whenever 20 vehicles are connected they hold all 20 ports and charge — often before the evening peak, and then leave the site idle later.

The optimizers choose which EV should hold a port in each slot, so they can move work into cheaper or higher-headroom slots and keep the site busier for longer at a lower peak. Ports, not energy, are the scarce resource: the sessions need about 343 port-hours at rated power and the windows supply about —.

Solar is drawn as available, not as delivered. Only — of the PV energy coincides with a connected EV, which is why the solar term barely separates the methods.

How the search works

Each EV carries four genes: a priority rank, how strongly it values urgency, how strongly it avoids expensive hours, and the fraction of its rated power it is willing to draw. GA and PSO both search that 400-gene vector, and a slot-by-slot decoder turns it into a feasible schedule.

Feasibility is structural, not penalised: the decoder never exceeds a connection window, a port count, a rated power or the grid cap. That is why every committed method reports zero violations, and why the optimizers cannot buy service by breaking a constraint.

Both optimizers are memetic: a fixed, exactly-counted budget of hill-climbing moves polishes the best genome after the evolutionary phase. The evaluation counts in the table are therefore measured, not estimated.

How the result is certified

The scenario is also solved as a mixed-integer linear program. It gives two things a metaheuristic cannot give itself: the maximum service any feasible schedule can deliver, and the cheapest schedule at each service level.

The MILP is the referee, not the competitor. For 100 sessions it solves in seconds, and that is exactly why it is useful here: it makes "97.0% of the best schedule known" a measured denominator instead of a claim. At ten times the sessions its port binaries grow with every feasible (EV, slot) pair while the search cost stays linear in the budget.

Every reference row carries its solver status, MIP gap and the SHA-256 of the inputs it was solved on. A row labelled optimal proved optimality.

Limitations worth saying out loud

  • The tariff is synthetic and illustrative, not a real supplier's price.
  • Solar is a modelled profile; only a small fraction of it coincides with connected EVs.
  • Battery stress is a relative charging-rate proxy, not a degradation prediction.
  • PSO fully charges fewer EVs than EDF (25 vs 61) while delivering more total energy. That is a distributional choice, visible in the file, not hidden by the headline.
  • Live runs use a few hundred to ~1,200 evaluations; the committed results used 12,680 (GA) and 13,030 (PSO). Live numbers will not match the committed table exactly.
  • The scenario is one 100-EV office site. The paper's scale argument is about search cost, not about a claim that MILP is beaten at this size.
